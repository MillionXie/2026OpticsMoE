# ABO time-separated capture: low-signal finding

## 2026-09-28 recovery and fixed reevaluation

After the manual SLM players were closed, automatic single-frame checks recovered calibrated CCD p99=124 at the first router and p99=57 at the final stage. The earlier five complete stages (2400 frames each) were preserved, and only `language_global` was recaptured into a separate directory. Its 2400 frames passed the per-batch p99>=15 guard, producing a complete six-stage set without the old dark final-stage frames.

The frozen readout and 4096-parameter electronic adapter were replayed once on the resulting 1600 physical gallery and 800 physical queries; no fitting or ratio selection occurred. The fixed fused R@1/R@5/R@10/MRR are **0.7625/0.8975/0.93375/0.824998**. The selected readout alone gives **0.5975/0.7975/0.8625/0.689084**. The acquisition script's original unadapted readout gives R@1 **0.6400**; this is a different readout and not a contradiction. A tensor-level comparison confirmed the selected model differs from the acquisition checkpoint only in `readout.projection.weight` and `.bias`, not in the upstream optical masks. Full per-query results are in [cpu_recovery_fixed_eval_report_20260928.json](cpu_recovery_fixed_eval_report_20260928.json). This is the same 800 query identities used during development and thus is not an independent generalization estimate. The recovered score is below the earlier 0.8000 fixed fusion; do not present it as meeting that threshold or as proof that the optical path is stable across time.

The historical dark-run diagnostic below is retained for provenance and must not be mixed with this recovered split-stage capture.

A read-only comparison of 48 paired TRAIN/gallery identities against the original healthy capture found first-stage vision-router median CCD p99 **79→134** and pixel PCC **0.855**; later vision-expert/global PCC medians were **0.279/0.268**. Language-router p99 was **38→61** with PCC **0.853**. The phase SHA matches at each stage, but **none of the 48 amplitude BMP SHA values match**, even at the first vision router. Therefore this paired comparison is not a strictly identical-input optical drift experiment; CPU-vs-original preprocessing/export differences and downstream feedback can contribute. It establishes a material capture-domain difference, not its sole cause. Full per-stage receipts and statistics are in [paired_train_ccd_diagnostic_20260928.json](paired_train_ccd_diagnostic_20260928.json). No query identities were used in this diagnosis.

An offline first-stage BMP reproduction for four TRAIN identities matched each run's receipt SHA exactly: the original capture used the CUDA-rendered BMP, while the recovery used the CPU-rendered BMP. Their pixel differences are limited to one gray level on **0.65–0.67%** of pixels (mean absolute difference <0.007/255; p99 absolute difference 0). Thus SHA inequality does **not** mean a large first-stage input change. The first-stage CCD brightness/pattern shift is much larger, though these observations alone cannot uniquely attribute it to optics, display, or camera state. See [cpu_gpu_bmp_equivalence_train4_20260928.json](cpu_gpu_bmp_equivalence_train4_20260928.json).

The same 1600 gallery and 800 query images were recaptured in six complete optical layers (14,400 CCD PNGs) with the original optical checkpoint `d4061a67…`, the same bounded BMP inputs, six phase BMPs, 400 µs/Gain X4/240 ms and the established ROI/orientation. All files and receipts are present. This is a repeat of the *same image identities*, not an unseen-image generalization test.

The frozen selected readout (`f413efa8…`) and frozen electronic adapter (`59f60149…`, 0.5 electronic share) were evaluated without fitting or selection on the repeat. R@1 changed from 0.75625 to 0.63875 for the six-layer physical path, and from 0.8000 to 0.7500 for the fixed fusion. The full per-query report is [repeat_fixed_report.json](repeat_fixed_report.json).

Those scores must not be interpreted as an ordinary robustness fluctuation: paired CCD images from 48 fixed IDs are nearly dark in every layer of the new run. At the **first vision router**, old/new p99 averaged 80.09/11.85, with image PCC 0.018. The first-stage discrepancy precedes any physical upstream recursion. The full stage comparison is [repeat_stage_compare_48ids.json](repeat_stage_compare_48ids.json). Example, native unrenormalized CCD frames: [original](ccd_signal_comparison/original.png), [repeat](ccd_signal_comparison/repeat_dark.png), [same-input recheck](ccd_signal_comparison/recheck_dark.png).

For the illustrated first sample, the old, repeat and later bounded one-frame recheck receipts have **identical amplitude BMP SHA** `797b21f1…`, **identical phase BMP SHA** `af4b297f…`, exposure 400 µs, Gain X4, 240 ms wait and `flip_v`. Their CCD p99 values are **81, 11, 12**. The recheck used the same four-sample input batch as the original run (a one-sample preparation was rejected before capture because its BMP hash differed). The recheck program then exited with a reporting `KeyError` because it read an aggregate receipt as a per-frame receipt; its persisted frame and per-frame JSON are valid and were read independently. No full capture was restarted.

The user reports that the optical path currently appears normal. This does not yet establish that light reaches the **configured high-speed camera ROI while the exact amplitude and phase BMPs are displayed**. The responsible point in the illumination/display/beam/ROI chain remains unresolved. Do not train on, normalize into, or promote the dark repeat as proof of model robustness; retain it for diagnosis. Do not run another full acquisition until a single-frame hardware check shows a substantive above-background pattern under the same settings.
